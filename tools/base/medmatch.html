The problem

Same prescription, different bodies.

Different bodies process drugs differently. The enzymes that clear a medication are built from your genes, so a dose that suits one person can underdeliver for another, or overwhelm them. Without that information, prescribing often begins as educated trial and error. And trial and error has a price.

See how MedMatch fixes this

Real examples

What MedMatch actually checks

Four findings straight from the MedMatch report, in the plain language your report is written in.

01

Aspirin resistance

Aspirin reduces clotting risk by affecting platelets. Some patients don't respond to this effect, called aspirin resistance. MedMatch identifies whether you're likely to be one of them.

02

Statin response

Predicts risk for statin-associated muscle pain, and whether a particular statin, like simvastatin or pravastatin, is likely to work well for you.

03

Tacrolimus dosing (CYP3A5)

For patients on tacrolimus, often after a transplant, this identifies whether you're likely to need a standard or a higher starting dose based on your CYP3A5 genotype.

04

Thiopurine metabolism

For patients prescribed 6-mercaptopurine or azathioprine, often for Crohn's disease, this identifies how your body is likely to metabolize these medications.
